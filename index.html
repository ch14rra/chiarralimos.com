About

A student who likes getting her hands into both petri dishes and prototypes.

I'm pursuing a B.S. in Biomedical Engineering at the University of California, Irvine, where I split my time between benchwork and build-space. In the Kessenbrock Lab at UCI Health's Chao Family Comprehensive Cancer Center, I work with single-cell RNA sequencing data and mouse colony genetics to understand how mutant tumor-suppressor proteins shape epithelial cells.

Outside the lab, I lead and build: directing a six-person team through the design of a low-cost hand orthosis for stroke rehabilitation, and engineering the suspension system for an award-winning fire-extinguishing rover. Earlier work took me from a stem-cell lab on the Central Coast to the streets of Oxnard, where I collected and analyzed survey data on youth economic mobility for the City Council.

What ties it together is a comfort with ambiguity — I like projects that start as an open question, whether that's "why do these cells behave this way" or "how do we get this rover over rough terrain."
